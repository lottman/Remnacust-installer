Keep it rolling.

Use these checks as reminders. Follow the bicycle and component manuals for procedures and torque values.

Air

Read the tyre’s permitted pressure range and use a gauge. Rider weight, tyre width and surface all affect your starting pressure. Inspect for cuts and embedded debris while the wheel turns.

Brakes

Check that each brake engages before its lever reaches the handlebar. Look for worn pads and damaged cables or hoses. Stop riding if braking is unreliable and ask a qualified mechanic.

Chain and contact points

Wipe off grit before adding lubricant. Keep oil away from braking surfaces. Check that the saddle and bars have not moved; use the correct torque procedure rather than tightening by feel.

Choose the route first

A city bicycle prioritises visibility, comfort and practical carrying. A gravel bicycle makes more room for varied surfaces. Neither label replaces a good test ride and properly fitted contact points.
