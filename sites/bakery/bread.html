A loaf for the day ahead.

Use these notes to choose a style, then follow a complete tested recipe for mixing, proofing and safe baking temperatures.

Everyday tin loaf

A supported dough is an approachable first bake. A little oil softens the crumb; a tin makes shaping forgiving. Weigh ingredients rather than scooping cups of flour.

Country-style round

A freestanding loaf needs enough dough strength to hold its shape. Stretch and fold gently. A supported proofing basket can help, but a floured cloth in a bowl also works.

Flatbread for supper

A small batch of flatbread is useful when you have less time. Keep the dough covered while it rests. Cook a test piece first so you can adjust the thickness and heat.

Keeping bread well

Cool on a rack so steam can escape. Keep a cut loaf wrapped for short storage, or slice and freeze portions. Do not keep a fresh loaf sealed while it is still warm.
